Ask anyone who has run a digital project what went wrong, and the answer is rarely "the design was bad" or "the code was buggy". It is almost always something that happened *between* the disciplines: a decision made in Figma that never reached the build ticket, a tracking plan written after launch instead of before, a developer who fixed a layout without telling the designer why.

## Where the gaps are

### Design → build

A handoff document is a lossy compression of a design. What survives is the layout; what usually dies is the intent — why the empty state is worded that way, which states are genuinely undefined, what the animation is supposed to communicate. The build team then fills those gaps with guesses, and the guesses are where the quality leaks.

### Build → growth

A site that launches without its analytics, SEO basics and conversion paths wired in is not finished — it is just unmeasured. Retrofitting a tracking plan after launch means every report until then is an estimate.

### Build → client

The client sees the preview link long after the decisions were made. Feedback that arrives at that point is not a change request; it is archaeology.

## What one team changes

When the same people design, build and then measure the result, the handoff document disappears — because there is no handoff. The person writing the copy knows how the CMS will store it. The person animating the hero knows the performance budget. Decisions stay attached to their reasons because the reason-holder is in the room the whole time.

This is how Zenova is [shaped as a studio](/about): one team from the first call to the ninetieth day, rather than a designer, then an agency, then a marketer. It is also why we [scope and price per project](/pricing) — a single accountable team cannot bill by the hour without billing the gaps.

## The honest caveat

A small team is a narrow one. Nobody is a specialist in everything, so the discipline is knowing what to decline: work that needs deep specialisation in five areas at once belongs with specialists. We would rather [turn down a brief](/about) than staff it thinly.
